87days

A Korean winter lasts 87 days on average. Here is what to do with them, on one page.

By the Korea Meteorological Administration's climate normals, winter starts on December 4 and ends on February 28, seven days shorter than under the previous normals (1981–2010). This page helps you decide when, where and what to do in those 87 days. Pick a time on the calendar, then check the reasons in the numbers below.

한국어로 보기. As of October 27, 2026. Regional winter festivals have not announced their 2027 dates yet, so the calendar shows their 2026 dates.

From mid-December, Seoul

The city is where winter has grown fastest

Seoul Winter Festa doubled in a single year, from 5.39 million to 10.98 million visitors. The Gwanghwamun Christmas Market went from 1.64 million to 3.57 million, and the Cheonggyecheon Lantern Festival drew 3.83 million, up 55% on the year before. This winter's Festa is listed for December 11, 2026 to January 31, 2027.

SoSkip the crowded weekends and the last week of the year. Walk from Gwanghwamun to Cheonggyecheon on a December weekday evening.

Mid-January to early February, Gangwon and Gyeongbuk

Ice festivals now open only when the weather allows

Ice-fishing festivals need the river to freeze thick enough. The Inje Smelt Festival has failed to open three years in a row, and the Andong Amsan Ice Festival was cancelled twice because the ice was too thin. The Pyeongchang Trout Festival has postponed its opening three years running. When they do open, people come: the Hwacheon Sancheoneo (mountain trout) Ice Festival had a record 1.86 million visitors in 2025.

January, the coldest month

In the same January, Daegwallyeong and Jeju are 16 degrees apart

January is the coldest month of the year, with a national average of −0.6°C. But regions differ a lot. The average January low is −12.2°C in Daegwallyeong and 3.7°C on Jeju. Go to Daegwallyeong for snow, and to Gangneung or Jeju for a winter you can walk around in. Daegwallyeong gets 13 snowy days in January; Seoul gets 7.1.

SoDecide what the January trip is for. For snow, head to Daegwallyeong; for walks by the sea, Gangneung or Jeju.

From late November

Skiers are coming back, but there are fewer ski resorts

Ski resort visits peaked at 6.86 million in the 2011/12 season, fell to 1.46 million during COVID-19, and recovered to 4.43 million in 2023/24. Over the same period the number of resorts dropped from 17 to 13. No resort has confirmed its opening day for this season yet; the only report is that Yongpyong is aiming for late November.

SoLast season, the big resorts opened between late November and early December. Buy a season pass during the discount period before opening is announced.

On a bitterly cold day

In January, hot springs saw the biggest jump in visitors

In January 2026, navigation searches for hot springs rose 246% on the year, more than museums or traditional markets. Hot spring visits nationwide reached 60.2 million in 2025, back to pre-COVID levels. The busiest is Onyang Hot Spring, and by province Gyeongbuk leads with 22.5%.

SoIf you go to Gangwon for an ice festival and a cold snap is forecast, switch that day to a nearby hot spring.

The morning of January 1

The first sunrise of the year is on the East Sea, and Gangneung draws the biggest crowd

On January 1, 2026, 300,000 people came to Gangneung alone to watch the sunrise: 130,000 at Gyeongpo, 110,000 at Jeongdongjin and 60,000 at Anmok and elsewhere. The sun rose first on Dokdo at 7:26, and on the mainland at Ganjeolgot in Ulsan at 7:31; Seoul saw it at 7:47. The Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute announces the 2027 sunrise times in December.

SoFor a quieter sunrise, pick Anmok over Gyeongpo in Gangneung, or Seongsan Ilchulbong on Jeju (about 20,000 people).
